# Kulstoflandskab – illustrationsbrief

Genereret med den indbyggede imagegen-funktion. Illustration uden etiketter;
de interaktive områder og tekster tilføjes separat i siden.

```text
Use case: scientific-educational.
Asset type: high-resolution base illustration for a Danish upper-secondary geography learning model of the carbon cycle. Create a completely new, exceptionally polished illustrated plate, NOT a web mockup.
Primary request: A beautiful, coherent coastal landscape with a geological cutaway. The quality should be that of a premium natural-history museum or modern geography textbook: accomplished environmental illustration, elegant composition, natural textures, finely observed vegetation, credible geology, subtle light and atmospheric depth. Detailed enough to reward inspection, but calm and legible at classroom projection size. Hand-painted digital gouache with precise scientific-illustration drawing, NOT clipart, flat SVG shapes, childish cartoon, generic 3D icons or a collection of separate panels.
Composition: wide landscape canvas, approximately 1600 by 1000. One continuous scene across the entire frame, viewed from a slightly elevated position with a clean front-facing geological cutaway. The land occupies the left 65 percent, the sea the right 35 percent. The surface of land and sea meet at a single credible coastline. The exposed underground fills the lower 35–40 percent. Nothing floats, every tree/building/rock has a believable physical connection to the terrain. Use the whole canvas, no white border.
Above ground: mature varied forest on rolling terrain on the left; individual crowns and branches with dappled light, moss, leaf litter and some exposed roots. A modest industrial complex with a couple of chimneys stands on its own flatter land just right of the forest, small relative to the landscape. Behind them is an attractive rugged mountain watershed in the middle, with weathered rocky ridges and subtle distant haze. A separate volcanic mountain behind and to the right of the watershed has a small crater and a restrained drifting pale steam/ash plume. The volcano must stand on the continental land, not float in the sea. A river originates in the mountain catchment, follows a naturally descending valley around the mountain foot and runs continuously to the sea. It must not flow uphill or arbitrarily across a cliff face. Show only a few fine rain streaks near the mountain, if helpful.
Sea: the water surface is a clearly horizontal line beginning at the coast. A graceful transparent cutaway of the blue-green sea reveals light attenuation with depth and the seabed sloping away from shore. Include a modest patch of realistic kelp/seaweed rooted to the seabed, fine suspended marine particles, and several recognisable small mollusc shells on the seabed farther right. No huge microorganisms, virus symbols or giant fish. Clean luminous water with restrained sunbeams.
Underground: all strata belong to the same geological cross-section, with continuous layered bedding naturally curving from beneath the forest through the coast to beneath the seabed. Beneath the forest clearly show a thin dark organic topsoil with leaf litter and branching fine roots. Deeper left, show a distinct coal seam embedded in sedimentary rock, recognisable by its dark irregular layered material. In the middle of the underground, show one credible gently folded porous reservoir beneath an impermeable arched caprock: a modest pale amber gas-bearing zone above a dark oil-bearing zone. These zones fill porous rock, NOT a giant empty cave or loose liquid bubble. Keep coal, oil and gas separate and visually distinguishable. Beneath the coast and sea on the right, include a broad pale limestone/chalk bed with fine bedding and occasional subtle fossil shell impressions. The sedimentary layers need natural rock texture and irregular bedding rather than masonry bricks or enormous checkerboards. A subtle deep dipping layer under the coastal region may suggest burial/subduction without becoming a second diagram.
Approximate compositional anchors to support later interactive labels: open atmosphere in upper left; forest at x 10–25%, y 35–45%; soil and roots at x 10–25%, y 50–62%; industrial chimneys at x 33–40%, y 28–43%; volcanic gases at x 60–70%, y 12–25%; river near x 60%, y 40–49%; sea water at x 78–95%, y 47–63%; marine living matter around x 75%, y 66%; shells around x 90%, y 74%; coal at x 8–25%, y 77–85%; oil/gas reservoir around x 37–48%, y 76–91%; limestone around x 61–77%, y 84–92%. These are approximate: overall physical coherence and visual beauty take precedence over rigid positioning.
Palette and lighting: deep forest greens with warmer sunlit foliage, muted sage-grey mountains, luminous teal and deep marine blue, warm ochre and umber sedimentary rock, ivory limestone, pale blue airy sky. Gentle clear daylight. Rich controlled texture and carefully defined material differences. Fine editorial finish, not photographic collage.
Text: absolutely NO text, labels, letters, numbers, arrows, question marks, boxes, chemical formulas, legend, watermark, border or UI. Later the app will add all interactive annotations. The illustration must stand beautifully on its own.
Scientific constraints: CO2 is invisible; visible chimney and volcanic plumes are environmental markers of emissions, not visible CO2 molecules. Diagram is schematically vertically exaggerated but the terrain, drainage, coastline, rooted vegetation, sedimentary strata and fossil-fuel trap must be mutually coherent. Do not imply that limestone turns into oil. No glowing lava lake in the entire mantle and no disconnected floating cutaway blocks.
```
